Know Before You Go

Practical Notes for British Travelers

◎
India e-Visa
British passport holders traveling to India for tourism generally apply for an eTourist e-Visa online in advance — plan to apply several weeks before departure. Requirements change from time to time, so always confirm current rules with official Indian government sources or your travel advisor before booking flights.
◎
Time Zone
India is roughly 4.5 hours ahead of the UK during the festival window, when both countries observe their respective daylight saving. It's a light enough shift that jet lag is barely a factor — a rare advantage over travelers coming from further west.
◎
Currency & Pricing
Packages are priced in U.S. dollars, the currency used across our journeys. Your card provider will convert to sterling automatically at checkout — worth a quick check with them beforehand so there are no surprises. Locally, India's currency is the rupee; cards are widely accepted and your concierge handles any cash needs on the ground.
◎
Power & Adapters
Good news for UK travelers: India and the UK both run on 230V, so your existing devices work without a voltage converter. You'll just need a plug adapter — India uses Type C/D/M sockets, not the UK's three-pin Type G.
◎
Language
English is widely spoken throughout Kolkata — a legacy of deep historical ties with Britain that you'll feel throughout the city. Every guest also travels with a fluent English-speaking guide and a dedicated cultural concierge.
◎
Weather in October
Expect warm, humid days around 29–33°C, with cooler, breezier evenings — especially at the pre-dawn Mahalaya ceremony. Pack lighter than you would for a British October, with one layer for early mornings.
Local Customs

A Few Notes on Etiquette

Small courtesies that go a long way — your guide will walk you through all of this in person, but it helps to arrive knowing the basics.

◎
Dress
Modest, breathable clothing is appreciated at pandals and temples — covered shoulders and knees, especially for the Ashtami Anjali offering.
◎
Photography
Most pandals welcome photography, but it's courteous to ask before photographing individual worshippers up close, particularly mid-ritual.
◎
Footwear
Shoes come off before entering many pandal sanctums — slip-ons will serve you far better than laces over five days of hopping.
◎
Greetings
A simple "Namaskar," said with palms together, is a warm and well-received greeting throughout the visit.
◎
If You're Invited Home
A small box from a sweet shop — rosogolla or sandesh — is a well-loved gesture if a Bengali family invites you in, which happens more often than you'd expect.
◎
Pace
The festival has its own rhythm — crowds build from late afternoon into the night. Mornings are calmer and often the best time for unhurried pandal visits.

The Calendar

The Five Days at a Glance

Durga Puja unfolds in a fixed sequence of ritual days — here's the shape of the week you're stepping into.

Day One
Shashthi
The Goddess's face is unveiled inside the pandal, and the city's decorations open to the public for the first time.
Day Two
Saptami
Formal worship begins, opened by the ritual bathing of the nine plants bound as the Goddess's companion.
Day Three
Ashtami
The most sacred day of the festival — the Anjali offering at dawn draws the largest crowds of the year.
Day Four
Navami
The final night of worship, often marked by all-night pandal hopping and the city's best street food.
Day Five
Vijaya Dashami
Sindur Khela and Bisarjan — the Goddess is bid farewell and carried back to the Ganges.
A Day in the Life · Ashtami Morning

Conch shells sound across the neighborhood before sunrise. By eight, you're standing shoulder to shoulder with thousands of Kolkatans for Anjali — the collective floral offering — before a late, leisurely breakfast of luchi and cholar dal.

One morning, illustrative of the festival's rhythm — your private guide shapes each day around your pace and interests.

Common Questions

From British Travelers

Do UK citizens need a visa for India?
Yes — most British travelers apply for an India e-Visa (eTourist visa) online before departure. We'll send you a checklist once you reserve your place, but always verify current requirements with official government sources, since rules do change.
How long is the flight from the UK to Kolkata?
The UK has the shortest routes of any Western gateway — as little as 11 to 14 hours from London, and up to around 16–17 hours from regional airports, usually with a single connection.
Is Kolkata safe for British travelers?
Kolkata is widely regarded as one of India's calmer, more welcoming major cities, and Durga Puja itself is a citywide family celebration. Groups are capped at eight guests and privately guided throughout, so you're never navigating the festival alone.
Will there be a language barrier?
No — English is widely spoken in Kolkata, and your guide and cultural concierge are both fluent English speakers with you for the full journey.
When should I book my flights?
Aim to book 3–4 months ahead for the best fares and seat availability into the festival window, and confirm your e-Visa is in hand before ticketing.
